\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{chap:eval}
The main handicap of our framework is its flexibility and large hyperparameter space. That has the consequence that for the same with the same drifts two different configurations of a perspective might have a very different performance in catchting the drifts or not catching the non-drifts.

In this chapter we will evaluate our framework from Chapter~\ref{chap:approach}. The goal is to show where is framework is strong and where it has limitations. In our evaluation we use the logs of the Padella benchmark~\ref{sec:eval:padella} and three sets of synthetic Rheon logs~\ref{sec:eval:rheon}. 

Since the output for a log of our framework is a drift signal over signal windows per perspective, we need to align it with log and real drifts points. In order to do our evaluation, we first formalize it and introduce an evaluation framework. Figure~\ref{fig:eval:example} shows an example of our evaluation framework and how it compares a drift signal and a log with drifts.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \emph{Runs:} A run describes for each log and evaluated perspective a configuration and a threshold $\tau$ for its drift scores of the signal windows.
	\item \emph{Signal windows:} The signal windows of a drift signal (Definition~\ref{def:approach:drift_signal}) are the unit of our evaluation. We only evaluate scored signal windows. A signal window is not scored if it has no reference, if it is the first or the last signal window and the log covers it only partially, or if it is thin, i.e., it has less than a quarter of the median number of rows.
	\item \emph{Detections:} A detection is a sequence $v_i, \dots, v_j$ of consecutive scored signal windows with $\mathit{score}_l \ge \tau$ for $i \le l \le j$ that cannot be extended. The detection lies in its first signal window $v_i$. Thus, a drift that keeps the drift scores high over several signal windows gives only one detection.
	\item \emph{Truth events and zones:} Each injected drift is a truth event of the perspective that is expected to catch it. If the drift point of a sudden drift lies in $v_k$, its zone is $v_{k-1}, v_k, v_{k+1}$. If a gradual drift starts in $v_s$ and ends in $v_e$, its zone is $v_{s-1}, \dots, v_{e+1}$.
	\item \emph{Counts:} A truth event is a True Positive (TP) if its zone contains a detection, otherwise it is a False Negative (FN). Every detection outside of the zones is a False Positive (FP), and we ignore further detections in a zone. Every scored signal window outside of the zones without a detection is a True Negative (TN). A perspective without truth events has no zones, so each of its detections is an FP.
	\item \emph{Metrics:} We sum up the counts of all logs of a run, also separately for each drift type and drift mode, and compute
	      \[
		      \mathit{Precision} = \frac{\mathit{TP}}{\mathit{TP} + \mathit{FP}}, \quad \mathit{Recall} = \frac{\mathit{TP}}{\mathit{TP} + \mathit{FN}} \quad\text{and}\quad \mathit{F1} = \frac{2 \cdot \mathit{Precision} \cdot \mathit{Recall}}{\mathit{Precision} + \mathit{Recall}}.
	      \]
\end{itemize}
TP and FN count drifts, FP counts detections and TN counts signal windows. The zone allows a detection one signal window before or after the drift (Section~\ref{sec:approach:drift_signals}). We compare two runs on the Padella benchmark and one run on each set of Rheon logs.

The logs and the configurations and thresholds of all runs are available on Google Drive\footnote{\url{https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1sie4iFTUNmHuyvMVoW8tktyvtoUzbmEp}}.

\section{Padella Benchmark}
\label{sec:eval:padella}
The Padella benchmark~\autocite{padella2026everything} consists of five real base logs (Table~\ref{tab:eval:padella:logs}), their five baselines without a drift and 29 logs with one injected drift each. Each injected drift changes one activity in 35 to 70\% of the cases. The benchmark has 15 sudden and 14 gradual drifts of three drift types:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \emph{Control-flow (\texttt{cf}):} Nine drifts delete, replace or insert an activity.
	\item \emph{Fast time (\texttt{time\_fast}):} Ten drifts multiply the duration of an activity by 0.1 to 0.5.
	\item \emph{Slow time (\texttt{time\_slow}):} Ten drifts multiply the duration of an activity by 2 to 6.
\end{itemize}
The time drifts only change the durations and not the waiting times. Figure~\ref{fig:eval:padella:perspectives} shows which perspective is expected to catch which drift type. The \texttt{cf} drifts are truth events of the intra-case perspective and the time drifts of the resource perspective. We only evaluate these two perspectives. We first present the results of both runs over all logs. Then, Sections~\ref{sec:eval:padella:cf} to~\ref{sec:eval:padella:time_slow} present the results per drift type and Sections~\ref{sec:eval:padella:sudden} and~\ref{sec:eval:padella:gradual} per drift mode.

The two runs only differ in their configurations and thresholds. Agents with our skill \texttt{kairo-drift-analyst} created those of Run~1 (Section~\ref{sec:impl:kairo:agent}), and we tuned them by hand for Run~2. Run~1 detects 19 of the 29 drifts, but has 75 FPs and only reaches an F1-score of 0.309. Run~2 detects 28 of the 29 drifts with 8 FPs and reaches an F1-score of 0.862 (Figure~\ref{fig:eval:padella:confusion} and Table~\ref{tab:eval:padella:metrics}). This shows the main handicap of our framework: on the same logs, two configurations of a perspective perform very differently.

Most of the FPs of Run~1 are not mistakes of the states. Real processes are not static even without an injected drift (first-order dynamics), and the baselines alone already get 13 FPs in Run~1. In the logs of Domestic declarations and Request for payment, the pre-approver also stops approving after December~21, 2017, and from January 2018 the administration and the budget owner approve instead. Run~1 detects this real change, but the benchmark does not know it, so each detection counts as an FP. 24 of the 30 FPs of Run~1 in these 14 logs lie at this turn of the year, whereas Run~2 has only one there (Figure~\ref{fig:eval:padella:timeline}). Thus, with suitable configurations, the states separate the injected drifts almost completely from the natural behavior of the real logs, but the results strongly depend on the configuration and the threshold.

\subsection{Control-Flow Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:padella:cf}
We expect the intra-case states to show the \texttt{cf} drifts directly, since these drifts change the activities of the cases (Figure~\ref{fig:approach:features_intra}). Both runs confirm this with a recall of 0.889 and 1.0, but Run~1 has 26 FPs and Run~2 only two. For example, in the Bank account closure log the activity \texttt{Pending Request for Reservation Closure} disappears on November~26, 2018. Run~1 only uses the current activity as feature, so that each of its 15 states stands for one activity. The share of the state of this activity drops from 13.6\% in the signal window from November~1 to 0.2\% in the signal window from January~24, 2019. Thus, the intra-case states reliably catch deleted, inserted and replaced activities, and with one state per activity they directly show which activity changes.

\subsection{Fast Time Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:padella:time_fast}
We expect the resource states to show the \texttt{time\_fast} drifts through the mean duration of the events of each resource, but not the intra-case states, since their features do not encode time. Run~1 only detects 5 of the 10 drifts with 22 FPs (F1-score 0.270), whereas Run~2 detects all ten with two FPs (F1-score 0.909). For example, in the Loan application log a sudden drift multiplies the duration of the activity \texttt{W\_Completeren aanvraag} by 0.1. The resource perspective of Run~1 only clusters the number of events per resource, which does not encode durations, and misses the drift. Run~2 uses the durations per resource and detects it. Thus, a drift that no feature of the configuration captures stays hidden in the states (Section~\ref{sec:approach:features}).

\subsection{Slow Time Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:padella:time_slow}
Since the \texttt{time\_slow} drifts multiply a duration by 2 to 6, we expect them to be easier to detect than the \texttt{time\_fast} drifts. This holds for Run~1 with a recall of 0.6 against 0.5. In Run~2, however, \texttt{time\_slow} is the weakest drift type with an F1-score of 0.783. Run~2 only misses the sudden \texttt{time\_slow} drift in the Domestic declarations log, which multiplies the duration of an activity by 4, and has four FPs on these logs. For example, in the Request for payment log a sudden drift on May~8, 2018, multiplies the duration of an activity by 5. Both perspectives of Run~1 detect the real change at the turn of the year 2017/18 instead. Since a run has only one configuration and one threshold per perspective, a stronger real change can hide an injected drift of the same perspective.

\subsection{Sudden Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:padella:sudden}
A sudden drift changes the state distribution from one signal window to the next (Section~\ref{sec:approach:drift_signals}). Therefore, we expect sudden drifts to be easier to detect. However, Run~1 only detects 9 of the 15 sudden drifts (F1-score 0.300) and Run~2 detects 14 (F1-score 0.824). Both runs detect all sudden \texttt{cf} drifts, but Run~1 misses six sudden time drifts. Moreover, the zone of a sudden drift only covers three signal windows, whereas the zone of a gradual drift covers 5.3 signal windows on average in Run~1. Thus, the longer zones favor the gradual drifts.

\subsection{Gradual Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:padella:gradual}
Run~1 detects 10 of the 14 gradual drifts (F1-score 0.400) and Run~2 all 14 (F1-score 0.903). Contrary to our expectation, gradual drifts therefore perform better than sudden drifts. For example, Run~1 misses the gradual \texttt{cf} drift in the Domestic declarations log with signal windows of 30 days and the previous signal window as reference. Run~2 detects it with signal windows of 56 days and the mean of the three previous signal windows as reference. In this example, longer signal windows with a reference over several signal windows show the gradual change better.

\section{Rheon Benchmark}
\label{sec:eval:rheon}
The Rheon logs contain drifts from all three perspectives (Chapter~\ref{chap:rheon}). Therefore, we evaluate all three perspectives on three sets of 15 logs each:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \emph{Single-drift logs:} Each log has one drift (Section~\ref{sec:eval:rheon:single}).
	\item \emph{Multi-drift logs:} Each log has two to four drifts from the same perspective (Section~\ref{sec:eval:rheon:multi}).
	\item \emph{Mixed-drift logs:} Each log has two to five drifts from different perspectives (Section~\ref{sec:eval:rheon:mixed}).
\end{itemize}
The expected perspective of a drift is the perspective of its drift type (Table~\ref{tab:rheon:drift_types}). Apart from the injected drifts, Rheon samples the logs from fixed distributions (Section~\ref{sec:rheon:generation}). However, some drift types also reach the features of other perspectives (Section~\ref{sec:approach:features}), and such reactions count as FPs. In fact, 29 of the 38 FPs of all three sets lie at most two signal windows next to a drift of another perspective, and most of the others lie at the start or the end of a log. Thus, a drift often shows in several perspectives, and its perspective only follows from comparing the states of all three perspectives. In each set, we first present the results over all logs, then per drift perspective and drift mode.

\subsection{Single-Drift Logs}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:single}
The first set consists of 15 logs with one drift each, five per perspective. Eight drifts are sudden and seven gradual. The logs have 3,254 to 124,021 cases, time spans from one month to five years, 4 to 22 activities and 3 to 60 resources. The run detects 14 of the 15 drifts with 12 FPs and reaches an F1-score of 0.683 (Table~\ref{tab:eval:rheon:single}). Nine of the 12 FPs are reactions of other perspectives to the drift of the log, and the other three lie at the end of a log. Thus, the states almost always detect a single drift in its expected perspective.

\subsubsection{Intra-Case Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:single:intra}
The run detects four of the five \texttt{control\_flow} drifts. Only the resource perspective reacts to the missed gradual drift, since its features include the shares of the activities and the handovers of the resources. All seven FPs of these logs come from the resource and the inter-case perspectives, which also react to the \texttt{control\_flow} drifts (Figures~\ref{fig:approach:features_resource} and~\ref{fig:approach:features_inter}). Thus, a detection in the resource or the inter-case perspective does not always indicate a drift of that perspective.

\subsubsection{Resource Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:single:resource}
The run detects all five resource drifts. The inter-case perspective also reacts to the \texttt{workload} drift with an FP, and the resource perspective reacts to the \texttt{arrival\_rate} drift. Both drift types change the counts in the same way, so that no feature distinguishes them (Sections~\ref{sec:approach:features:resource} and~\ref{sec:approach:features:inter}).

\subsubsection{Inter-Case Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:single:inter}
The run detects all five inter-case drifts, also the gradual \texttt{waiting\_time} drift, which doubles the mean waiting time over almost six months. In the logs of the three attribute drifts (\texttt{amount} and \texttt{region}), no perspective has an FP except one at the end of a log. Since attribute drifts only reach the attribute features, the states detect them clearly.

\subsubsection{Sudden Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:single:sudden}
The run detects all eight sudden drifts with seven FPs (F1-score 0.696). Six of these FPs are reactions of other perspectives to the drift of their log. Thus, the states detect every single sudden drift, and most FPs come from the other perspectives.

\subsubsection{Gradual Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:single:gradual}
The run detects six of the seven gradual drifts with five FPs (F1-score 0.667) and only misses one \texttt{control\_flow} drift. Thus, with a single drift per log, the F1-score hardly depends on the drift mode.

\subsection{Multi-Drift Logs}

The logs have 3,132 to 57,197 cases, time spans from six weeks to five years, 5 to 22 activities and 3 to 35 resources. We expect several drifts of one perspective to be harder to detect than a single drift. The run detects 25 of the 44 drifts with 13 FPs and reaches an F1-score of 0.610 (Table~\ref{tab:eval:rheon:multi}). Compared to the single-drift logs, the recall drops from 0.933 to 0.568. 11 of the 13 FPs are reactions of perspectives without a drift of their own to a drift in the log.

\label{sec:eval:rheon:multi:intra}

In four of the five logs, the resource perspective also reacts to a \texttt{control\_flow} drift, and in three of them also the inter-case perspective. Each of these reactions counts as an FP. Thus, several control-flow drifts in one log are much harder to detect than a single one with a recall of 0.8.

\subsubsection{Resource Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:multi:resource}
The run fully detects the logs with only \texttt{pool\_size} or only \texttt{reassignment} drifts. However, it misses one gradual \texttt{reassignment} drift, all three \texttt{workload} drifts and both \texttt{duration} drifts. The two \texttt{duration} drifts only affect two of the 10 and two of the 20 resources. Thus, the resource states here do not show \texttt{workload} drifts and \texttt{duration} drifts of few resources.

\subsubsection{Inter-Case Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:multi:inter}
The run detects all five \texttt{region} drifts. It misses five drifts in two logs, among them three of the five \texttt{waiting\_time} drifts. In one log, the resource perspective reacts to an \texttt{arrival\_rate} and a \texttt{waiting\_time} drift, since its features include the counts and the mean waiting time until the resource (Figure~\ref{fig:approach:features_resource}). Thus, the inter-case states only partly separate several \texttt{waiting\_time} drifts in one log.

\subsubsection{Sudden Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:multi:sudden}
The logs with only sudden drifts reach an F1-score of 0.759. Both FNs come from one small log with four \texttt{control\_flow} drifts in three months. In the other logs with only sudden drifts, the run detects all drifts.

\subsubsection{Gradual Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:multi:gradual}
In the only log with only gradual drifts, the run misses both \texttt{control\_flow} drifts (F1-score 0), and the logs with both drift modes reach an F1-score of 0.560. Overall, the run only detects four of the 14 gradual drifts. Thus, gradual drifts between other drifts of the same perspective mostly stay undetected here.

\subsection{Mixed-Drift Logs}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:mixed}
The third set consists of 15 logs with two to five drifts from different perspectives, with 12 intra-case, 15 resource and 17 inter-case drifts in total, 29 of them sudden and 15 gradual. In five logs, drifts of different perspectives share their drift point or drift window. The logs have 2,860 to 42,192 cases, time spans from one month to five years, 4 to 25 activities and 3 to 45 resources. We expect simultaneous drifts of different perspectives to hide each other. The results do not confirm this: the run detects all twelve drifts of the five logs with simultaneous drifts. Overall, it detects 36 of the 44 drifts with 13 FPs and reaches the highest F1-score of the three sets with 0.774 (Table~\ref{tab:eval:rheon:mixed}). Since each perspective has its own states, our framework separates simultaneous drifts of different perspectives.

\label{sec:eval:rheon:mixed:intra}
The intra-case perspective has no FP in any of the 15 logs, although the logs contain 32 drifts of other perspectives. The run only misses one of the twelve \texttt{control\_flow} drifts. Thus, the intra-case states are specific: a detection here indicates a control-flow drift.

\subsubsection{Resource Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:mixed:resource}
The run detects all six \texttt{reassignment} drifts, also next to a simultaneous \texttt{control\_flow} drift. However, it misses two \texttt{duration}, two \texttt{workload} and one \texttt{pool\_size} drift. Moreover, the resource perspective has 10 of the 13 FPs, six of them next to a \texttt{control\_flow} drift and three at the start or the end of a log. Thus, in logs with several drifts the resource states rarely show \texttt{duration} and \texttt{workload} drifts, and they also react to control-flow drifts.

\subsubsection{Inter-Case Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:mixed:inter}
The run detects all \texttt{region}, \texttt{waiting\_time} and \texttt{arrival\_rate} drifts and only misses two sudden \texttt{amount} drifts. In two logs, it detects the \texttt{arrival\_rate} drift but not the \texttt{workload} drift. Since both drift types change the same counts, a change of the volume mainly shows in the inter-case states here.

\subsubsection{Sudden Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:mixed:sudden}
The logs with only sudden drifts reach an F1-score of 0.815. Three of these logs contain simultaneous sudden drifts, which do not hide each other.

\subsubsection{Gradual Drifts}
\label{sec:eval:rheon:mixed:gradual}
Both logs with only gradual drifts contain simultaneous drifts of several perspectives. The run detects all of them (F1-score 0.833). Thus, simultaneous gradual drifts do not hide each other either.
